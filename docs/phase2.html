Phase 2 — Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct:
the layer-selection lesson

On a model where every layer classifies harmful vs harmless perfectly, AUC-based layer selection fails. Bypass-gap-based layer selection recovers the layer where ablation actually changes behavior — and plain diff-of-means then extracts the causal refusal direction there.

Anshul Singhle May 2026 [index] [← Phase 1] [code]
This is the Qwen half of the project. Phase 1 (phase1.html) is the Gemma evidence; the top-level index states the unified thesis across both phases. This page tells the L14→L22 arc: an inert headline at the AUC-selected layer, an observation that AUC is saturated on Qwen, a switch to bypass-gap-based layer-selection (ranking layers by which one's direction, when ablated, drops refusal the most — a causal-effect criterion instead of a classification one), and a clean causal result at the layer that selection picks out. The portable lesson — pick layers by causal effect on behavior, not by classification accuracy — is the practical-teeth contribution of this phase. Throughout: ablate means zero the direction out of the residual stream during generation.

1. Setup: Qwen, the matched contrastive set, and the AUC observation

The Phase 1 protocol on Gemma extracts a refusal direction at L13 — picked because a linear probe trained to classify harmful vs harmless prompts peaks in AUC there. The same recipe ported to Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct starts the same way: train a probe at each of Qwen's 36 layers on a matched contrastive set, find the peak, extract a direction there, ablate, measure.

The matched contrastive set (data/code_contrastive_matched.jsonl) is 40 HarmBench-cybercrime harmful prompts paired with 40 hand-authored defensive equivalents — same imperative form, same domain vocabulary, length-matched. Vocabulary is controlled to TF-IDF unigram 5-fold-CV AUC ≈ 0.50 (chance) so a "direction" that separates the two classes can't just be a word-frequency direction.

The first surprise on Qwen is that the linear-probe sweep gives no AUC peak to speak of: every layer classifies the matched contrast at AUC ≥ 0.994, including the embedding layer L0 (AUC = 0.9996). Separability is saturated. Picking "the AUC-best layer" is effectively picking arbitrarily — in our run that fell on L14 because it was the first layer to hit float-1.000.

2. The L14 null: AUC picked the wrong layer

Ablating the diff-of-means direction d̂ at L14 on the matched contrast drops refusal from 1.00 to 0.97 — essentially no change. Random-direction ablation at the same layer also leaves refusal at baseline. On the substring scorer, on the dual-judge, and on a continuous addition-sweep follow-up at multiple layers × coefficients, the L14 direction does very little: small additive lift in a narrow operating band, no clean bypass under ablation.

The reflex reading was that diff-of-means doesn't work on Qwen — that refusal on this model isn't single-direction-mediated and would require a gradient method (e.g. RDO) to recover. That reading turned out to be premature. The actual failure was upstream: AUC isn't a useful layer-selection criterion when AUC is saturated everywhere. Linear separability tells you the residual stream contains some direction along which harmful vs harmless are linearly distinguishable; on a saturated model that's true at every depth, so it tells you nothing about which direction matters causally.

3. The switch: bypass-gap-based layer selection

The alternative criterion, used internally by Wollschläger et al.'s ICML 2025 select_direction step, is to rank candidate (layer, token-position) cells by bypass gap: how much does ablating the direction at that cell drop the refusal-token logit on a held-out harmful set? Layers with high bypass-gap are layers where the direction changes the behavior, not just where it classifies.

Running this sweep on the matched contrast — 5 token positions × 36 layers = 180 cells — surfaces a candidate region around L20–L24 at the end-of-instruction token positions (positions −4 and −1 in the ChatML template). The full n_pos × n_layer bypass-gap profile makes the contrast vivid: most cells score near zero, a tight cluster around L22 scores ten times higher.

Re-running the same Phase 1 protocol — extract d̂ by diff-of-means, ablate during generation, dual-judge — at the bypass-gap-selected cells on the matched contrast, with random-direction specificity controls at the same cells, is what the headline section reports.

4. The headline result at L22

At L22 on the matched contrast, ablating (zero out the direction during generation) d̂ turns Qwen from refusing harmful requests into writing the requested exploit code, while ablating a random direction at the same cell leaves refusal at baseline. The direction is specific, and the layer isn't just fragile.

5. Why AUC fails, mechanistically — backwards-decomposition cosines

The headline so far is empirical: AUC picked an inert layer, bypass-gap picked the causal one. To make that mechanistic — to say why AUC can't find the mechanism on saturated models — we look at the directions the two criteria produce and ask whether they're related at all in residual-stream geometry.

Same model, different selection criteria → substantially different directions

cos(d̂_L22_pos-1, d̂_L22_pos-4)+0.669Same model, two extraction positions, same matched data — clearly the same subspace. Internal consistency.
cos(d̂_L22, d̂_L14_matched)+0.14 to +0.15Same data, two layer-selection criteria — AUC and bypass-gap find substantially different directions.
cos(d̂_L22, d̂_L14_old)−0.02L22 (bypass-gap, matched contrast) vs L14 on the original lexically-confounded code_contrastive set — essentially unrelated.

The two layer-selection criteria don't converge: they find directions with cosine ≈ 0.14 on the same data. That's a small overlap, not zero — but the directions are not the same thing. The L14 direction lives mostly in one subspace; the L22 direction lives mostly in another.

Future work: the L0-separability capstone

The cosines above show that AUC-based and bypass-gap-based layer-selection find substantially different directions on the same data — that's the empirical result. The mechanistic capstone would be one more cosine: cos(d̂_L22, d̂_L0_matched), where d̂_L0_matched is the diff-of-means direction extracted at the embedding layer L0 on the same matched contrast. If that cosine is near zero, the L22 causal direction is nearly perpendicular to the L0-separability subspace — and since AUC ranks directions by classification utility, the orthogonality would explain why AUC fails to find the causal direction on saturated models. That extension is queued as a follow-up; the cosines above are sufficient to support the empirical headline ("AUC and bypass-gap find substantially different directions") and the methodological recipe in §7. The structural-orthogonality explanation gets stated only once the L0 cosine is in.

6. The pre-registered stance bound

The matched contrast was designed to control vocabulary and length confounds: 5-fold-CV TF-IDF AUC ≈ 0.50, length medians equal, no pair more than 20 characters apart, paired-set CV artifact mechanism verified by shuffle control. What it cannot control is attacker-vs-defender stance: a contrastive set that pairs "write the harmful code" against "write the defensive code" inherently mixes refusal (the model declines because the request is harmful) with stance (the model declines because it's being asked to act as attacker rather than defender).

A direction that separates these two classes is a direction along which "act as attacker" and "act as defender" pull apart. That direction includes refusal — the matched-test ablation collapses refusal as reported — but it isolates refusal-or-stance, not refusal alone. This is a real limit of the contrastive set, pre-registered as such, not a hedge. The next experimental design that would disentangle the two is a defender-only refusal contrast (where the model refuses some defensive requests too, isolating refusal from role) or a gradient extraction method that doesn't rely on a single contrastive set's labels.

The stance bound is orthogonal to the layer-selection finding. Whether the direction encodes refusal alone or refusal-or-stance, the lesson "pick the layer by causal effect, not by classification accuracy, on saturated-separability models" stands.

7. Methodological summary, portable to the next model

The protocol that the Qwen arc converges on, stated as a recipe for the next saturated-separability model someone tries this on:

  1. Build a length-and-vocabulary-controlled matched contrastive set. Confounds at the contrast level lead to confounded directions; control what you can and pre-register what you can't.
  2. Run a layer-wise AUC sweep. If AUC is saturated (≥ ~0.99 across most layers including early ones), AUC cannot pick your layer. Treat this as the diagnostic — if separability is cheap everywhere, do not pick by separability.
  3. Sweep candidate (layer, position) cells, extract d̂ by diff-of-means at each, rank by bypass-gap (refusal-token logit drop under ablation on a held-out harmful val set). The candidate set of 180 cells is cheap to enumerate — diff-of-means is centroid subtraction; only the ranking step needs a forward pass per cell.
  4. At the top-ranked cell(s), run a full ablation evaluation: dual-judge scoring on held-out harmful, random-direction specificity control at the same cell across multiple seeds, mean-chars coherence sentinel. The random control is load-bearing — without it, "ablation collapses refusal" is consistent with "this layer is fragile to any perturbation." With it (random leaves refusal at baseline), the result is direction-specific.
  5. If desired (optional), confirm with a gradient extractor like RDO at the same layer. Cosine between the gradient and statistical directions at the same layer is the inter-method consistency check. Not load-bearing — diff-of-means already gave the causal direction — but tightens the story.

The portable claim. On a saturated-separability model, diff-of-means is fine as an extractor — what changes is the layer-selection criterion. Switching from AUC to bypass-gap moves the recipe from "doesn't find the causal direction" to "finds it cleanly" on a model where the L14-vs-L22 distinction was invisible from classification accuracy.

8. What's deferred

RDO confirmation cosine. Wollschläger 2025's gradient extractor (RDO) optimizes a direction at a fixed layer such that ablating it maximally drops refusal. Running RDO at L22 on the same matched contrast would give a d_rdo direction and the inter-method consistency cosine cos(d_rdo, d̂_L22). Expectation: high (the two methods agree once the layer is causally selected). This is an appendix-tightening, not a required experiment — the diff-of-means result already establishes the causal direction at L22.

Larger matched contrastive set. The current 40-pair set is the floor at which the headline result holds. Doubling or tripling the set would tighten the natural-scale measurement and let the stance confound be partially disentangled by adding defender-vs-defender pairs.

Other models. The natural next step is to run the same recipe on a third saturated-separability model — e.g. a Qwen 7B variant, or Phi-3 — to check whether the AUC-vs-bypass-gap divergence is reproducible across model families. Out of scope for this writeup.
